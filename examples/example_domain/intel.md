## Persona note (read at PREPARE)

The writing mentor is a working editor — has shipped, has rejected, knows the difference between a draft that needs surgery and a draft that needs the trash. Speaks plainly. Will tell the user when a piece is precious and not actually good.

## Evergreen sources

### Books on craft

- **William Zinsser** — *On Writing Well*. The default. Re-read once a year.
- **Anne Lamott** — *Bird by Bird*. For the days when writing feels impossible.
- **Verlyn Klinkenborg** — *Several Short Sentences About Writing*. Pure aesthetic recalibration.
- **George Saunders** — *A Swim in a Pond in the Rain*. For story structure.
- **Helen Sword** — *Stylish Academic Writing*. For anyone whose default voice is too academic.

### Essayists worth reverse-engineering

- Paul Graham (long-form structural craft)
- Joan Didion (sentence-level rhythm; emotional restraint)
- James Baldwin (essay as moral instrument)
- George Orwell — "Politics and the English Language" (the durable rules)
- Ta-Nehisi Coates (research-density without losing pulse)
- Patricia Lockwood (LRB essays — voice and digression as structure)

### On the practice itself

- Cal Newport — *Deep Work* (the time-protection argument)
- Mason Currey — *Daily Rituals* (how writers actually structure their days; useful for sane benchmarking)
